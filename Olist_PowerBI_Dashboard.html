Root Cause Ranking

The most important factors associated with low review scores, ranked so primary drivers can be distinguished from secondary contributors. This is descriptive/associative — not proof of causation from observational data.

Method: a standardized logistic regression predicts the probability of a low review (≤2★, baseline rate 13.2%) from nine order-level factors. Coefficients are on a common (standardized) scale, so bar length directly compares each factor's effect size.

PRIMARYOrder delivered late — by far the largest effect of any factor tested, consistent across every cut of the data (region, category, time period).
SECONDARYOrder complexity (# items, # distinct sellers) and cross-state shipping — plausibly act by raising the chance of a late delivery, rather than being independent root causes.

Recommendations

What Olist should prioritize to improve the platform experience as it continues to scale, ranked by the strength of evidence behind each.

1

Protect delivery timing during demand peaks

Primary driver
  • Build seasonal/promotional-period capacity plans with logistics partners ahead of known peaks (Nov/Black Friday is a clear strain point in the data).
  • Set more conservative estimated-delivery-date promises during high-volume periods and for high-risk regions/categories.
  • Monitor delay days as a leading indicator in near-real-time, not just review score after the fact.
2

Reduce the cross-state logistics penalty

Secondary, structural
  • Recruit and onboard sellers in underserved regions (North/Northeast) to shorten shipping distances for the customers most affected.
  • Explore regional fulfillment/consolidation hubs outside São Paulo for the states with the highest late rates (AL, MA, SE, PI, CE).
3

Target category-specific delivery risk

Secondary, contributing
  • Work with sellers in office_furniture and bed_bath_table on packaging and carrier-selection standards, since delivery risk — not price or listing quality — appears to drive their below-average reviews.
4

Build a retention program

Growth opportunity
  • Repeat-purchase rate (3.1%) is low for a marketplace; first-time and repeat customers report similar satisfaction, so the gap is in return behavior, not experience quality — a separate lever from the fixes above.
What NOT to prioritize: payment method / installment options (no measurable link to satisfaction) and broad, category-agnostic pricing or listing-quality initiatives (not supported as satisfaction levers by this data).
Source: Brazilian E-Commerce Public Dataset by Olist (Kaggle, CC BY-NC-SA). Analysis is associative, not causal — a well-evidenced case for what is likely driving outcomes, built from real, unlabeled operational data.
